What you are looking at

The representation

The scene is a cloud of 3D Gaussians: soft ellipsoids, each with a position, three scales, a rotation, a colour and an opacity. There is no mesh and no texture at render time, only those numbers.

What happens when you click

For every Gaussian: build its 3D covariance from scale and rotation, project it through the camera into a screen-space ellipse, sort by depth, then blend front to back into the pixels it touches. That is the whole renderer, in about sixty lines of PyTorch, and it is differentiable, so the same code drives training.

What training did

The gnome began as 10,000 points sampled from a CC0 mesh, then had every parameter jittered. Two thousand gradient steps against eight rendered views pulled it back; error on four unseen views fell from 8.6e-3 to 2.8e-4. Select initial to see the starting point. The robot is a 31-Gaussian scene trained on a laptop CPU.

What this is not

A reconstruction pipeline. Cameras are known, targets are synthetic, the starting point is a perturbation of the answer, and the renderer is dense rather than the paper's tiled rasteriser. It exists to make the maths inspectable, not to compete with the original.
